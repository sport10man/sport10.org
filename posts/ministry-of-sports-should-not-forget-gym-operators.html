Ministry of Sports Should not Forget Gym Operators

Original post in Chinese. (2024)

Exciting news about the Paris Olympics has arrived, and the public is thrilled beyond words. President Lai's pre‑election proposal to upgrade the Sports Administration has also gained momentum for accelerated development. While the nation celebrates the prospect of winning medals in competitive sports, it often forgets the thousands of gyms that are closely tied to everyday life.

"Popularizing competitive sports" sounds simple, but behind it are countless gym professionals who receive little policy support and are frequently misunderstood. Elite athletes are few, and those who become coaches are even rarer. Many young graduates from sports programs enter the fitness service industry as their first career step. This path offers relatively good average earnings, belongs to a lifestyle sector with high growth potential, and allows them to reach a broad audience. The two waves of gym expansion before and after the pandemic have also drawn many middle‑aged people into fitness training, whether for employment or for professional services.

However, gyms in Taiwan face a variety of unique challenges. First, regulatory flexibility is lacking; for example, many studios struggle with fire‑safety regulations, and older urban planning schemes never accounted for the survival of small studios. Second, fitness providers deliver highly customized instruction rather than the large‑class model typical of traditional tutoring, and demand has not waned—in fact, it has grown despite the low birth‑rate era.

Third, the trend toward regional and community‑based gyms is increasing, with some even functioning as neighborhood community centers. Fourth, the certification system for sports training differs entirely from that of single‑sport associations; the two credentialing systems were designed for different purposes and need to coexist.

Fifth, there remain many unqualified players, leading to frequent consumer disputes. Policymakers must proactively understand these issues at the source of policy discussion, avoiding premature lock‑ins that overlook the vitality of the workforce and the market's real conditions.

Many middle‑aged citizens are rediscovering competitive sports and learning about the Paris Olympics through personal trainers and gyms. In this educational role, gyms and coaches provide immense societal benefit. Moreover, the large workforce extends into sports injury prevention and other professional medical services. As the Sports Administration is upgraded, the philosophy that sport is part of daily life should be put into practice. The first step is not to forget the gym owners who live next door.
